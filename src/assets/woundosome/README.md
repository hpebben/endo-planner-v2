# Original patent foot arterial reference model

The wound selector uses original SVG artwork generated by
`src/components/FootAngiographicModel.jsx` from the connected arterial graph in
`src/data/footArterialModel.js`. It contains no patient pixels and no extracted
figures. The former Patrone patient angiogram PNGs have been removed.

The model depicts a fully patent, classical left-foot arterial configuration.
It is an angiographic-style educational reference, not acquired angiography,
a patient-specific reconstruction, or a claim that this branching pattern is
universal. Relative calibers and coordinates are drawing units, not measured
diameters or morphometric data. Faint original bone/soft-tissue outlines provide
orientation; they are schematic context rather than a diagnostic bone atlas.

## Anatomical sources

- Manzi et al., *Vascular Imaging of the Foot: The First Step toward Endovascular
  Recanalization*, RadioGraphics (2011), 31:1623–1636,
  https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/rg.316115511.
  The complete anatomical sections and Figures 3, 5, 7–10 inform the anterior,
  posterior, peroneal and pedal–plantar connections. Figures 2–3 inform projection
  and first/fifth-ray orientation. No source figures are redistributed.
- Higashimori, *Angiography and Endovascular Therapy for Below-the-Knee Artery
  Disease* (2017), https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/53810.
  The foot anatomy sections corroborate the tarsal/arcuate connections, plantar
  arch, four intermetatarsal arteries and distal digital branching.

## Arterial relationships represented

- Anterior tibial artery continues as dorsalis pedis across the dorsal foot.
  Medial/lateral malleolar and tarsal branches are included. The arcuate artery
  crosses the metatarsal bases and joins the lateral tarsal artery; it supplies
  dorsal metatarsal arteries 2–4. The first dorsal metatarsal arises from DP.
- The deep plantar branch of DP passes through the **proximal first
  intermetatarsal space** to join the deep plantar arch. It is not placed at the
  toe tips. This is the main pedal–plantar loop connection.
- Posterior tibial passes behind the medial ankle, gives medial calcaneal
  branches, then divides into medial and lateral plantar arteries. Lateral
  plantar runs toward the fifth metatarsal base, then turns **medially** into
  the deep plantar arch. Four plantar metatarsal arteries course distally in
  the interspaces and divide near the metatarsal heads into proper digital
  branches. Both sides of all five toes receive digital branches.
- The medial plantar superficial course supplies the medial instep; its deep
  branch communicates with the first plantar metatarsal. The medial tarsal–
  medial plantar superficial connection corresponds to Manzi's "deep pedal arch", distinct
  from the deep **plantar** arch. Proximal and distal intermetatarsal perforators
  connect dorsal and plantar circulations.
- The peroneal terminates around the ankle, with an anterior perforating
  connection, a posterior tibial communicating branch and lateral calcaneal
  branches. It is not drawn as an additional forefoot inflow trunk. Medial heel
  supply arises from posterior tibial; lateral heel supply from peroneal.

This selects a complete reference configuration from known normal variants.
It does not add enlarged disease-related collateral channels. Arteries have
continuous lumens without occlusion, stenosis, plaques or treatment hardware.

## One spatial model, two projections

All arterial junctions are shared node IDs. The x-axis runs medial to lateral,
y heel to toes and z plantar to dorsal. AP/dorsoplantar and lateral oblique use
orthonormal projection axes; both project the exact same graph, including its
calibers and junctions. The AP view tilts 30 degrees; the lateral oblique rotates
15 degrees from lateral, with 12 degrees of elevation and a 25-degree image-plane
rotation for presentation. These are illustrative reference projections, not
a calibrated acquisition protocol. The whole forefoot is visible in both.

Wound overlays use the same spatial coordinates. Hallux/first ray is medial;
option 7 is on the lateral fifth-ray side. Colored areas are approximate wound
surface locations, not arterial lumens or sharp perfusion territories.
Dorsal/plantar locations overlap in AP. Medial/lateral heel surfaces overlap
laterally, so separate callouts lead to the same heel area. Saved wound IDs,
their colors and the TAP logic remain unchanged.

Patrone et al. (2024), DOI 10.1177/15266028241231745, remains the clinical
woundosome-concept reference, independently of the original anatomy artwork.
