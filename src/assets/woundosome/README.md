## Anatomical basis and orientation

Manzi et al., *Vascular Imaging of the Foot: The First Step toward Endovascular
Recanalization*, RadioGraphics (2011), 31:1623–1636,
https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/rg.316115511, supplies the anatomical basis.
Figures 2–3 identify the first interspace and anterior circulation; Figures 5–6
identify the medial instep, lateral plantar surface/forefoot and medial heel;
Figure 7 identifies the peroneal ankle and lateral heel supply. The hallux can
receive anterior or posterior inflow, including the lateral plantar artery via
the plantar arch and first plantar metatarsal artery (pp. 1629–1631).
No figures from the RadioGraphics article are redistributed in these assets.

The PNGs have not been mirrored or rotated. In the AP panel (382 × 500 SVG
coordinates), the hallux tip is at approximately (193, 470), and the fifth-ray
tip at (367, 298). The medial plantar instep is on the first-ray side, left of
the midfoot; the lateral plantar surface is on the fifth-ray side, upper-right
of the midfoot. These image landmarks anchor the overlays and regression tests.
The direction in which the lateral plantar artery turns into the plantar arch
must not be confused with the lateral surface of the foot.

The images show the published reference left foot after revascularization. They
are educational reference images, not the user's case imaging and not a normal
arterial atlas. Colored markers and approximate projected wound locations are
separate SVG overlays based on the anatomical relationships above. They do not
indicate proven perfusion territories,
vessel patency, or sharp angiosome boundaries. Dorsal/plantar locations overlap
in the AP projection; medial/lateral heel locations overlap in the lateral
projection. Both heel callouts lead to the same projected area, since a lateral
image cannot determine the medial/lateral side of a wound. The AP field does not
adequately show the heel and malleoli. The lateral field crops the toes: toe and
side-specific instep markers appear only in AP, rather than on guessed locations
outside the lateral image. All nine locations remain selectable in the key.
Patrone describes these panels as AP and latero-lateral; they are not relabeled
as the standard lateral-oblique projection recommended by Manzi et al.
